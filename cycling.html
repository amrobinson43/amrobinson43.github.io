Bike-Fit Cockpit Matcher

Move your riding position from one bike to another by putting the front end, meaning your hoods, in the same place in space relative to the bottom bracket.

Interactive Tool

Match the front end of a new bike to your current fit

Every point on a bike fit can be described by where it sits relative to the bottom bracket (BB), the fixed origin your body works around. This tool takes the geometry of two bikes and computes exactly where each one places your hood contact point (x is how far forward, y is how far up, both measured from the BB). It then finds the stem length, spacer stack, and stem angle that bring a new bike's hoods to the same point as your current bike, so your position carries over unchanged.

1Set the saddle first

Put the saddle in the same XY relative to the BB as your current bike, meaning the same effective height and setback, or wherever you want it. The BB anchors both ends, so once the saddle matches, matching the front end reproduces your whole position.

2Match the front end

Enter both bikes below. The tool reports each bike's hood XY from the BB and the gap between them, then solves for the stem and spacers that close that gap.

3Read the diagram

The to-scale drawing shows every input in space, including reach, stack, spacers, stem, handlebar rise, and bar reach, and overlays both bikes so you can see the difference directly.

New: handlebar rise / stack. Riser road bars are increasingly common. The handlebar rise input lifts the hood contact point vertically above the bar clamp, so bars with built-in rise are modeled correctly instead of being treated as flat.

Auto-match Bike B to Bike A

Search space: stem length 70 to 140 mm in 5 mm steps, 0 to 16 spacers, and stem angle from −17° to +17°. Choose what Bike B is allowed to change. Leave both off for an integrated one-piece cockpit, or turn on spacers if you have steerer to work with.

Model & assumptions
  • Simplified 2D side view. x is forward, y is up, and the origin is the bottom bracket.
  • Frame stack and reach are taken as the top of the head tube (as on standard geometry charts).
  • Spacers stack along the steerer axis; stem angle is measured relative to the steerer (0° = perpendicular to it), matching how stems are specced.
  • The hood point is the bar reach plus shifter/lever reach forward of the bar clamp, raised by the handlebar rise.
  • Ignored for simplicity: bar drop, hood rotation, headset/top-cap stack, and clamp stack height. Use it to match bikes to each other, not as an absolute fit study.
